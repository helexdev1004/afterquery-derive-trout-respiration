A fish physiology lab has run seventeen juvenile brown trout through a single night of intermittent-flow respirometry, and the night now has to be worked up. The trout had been acclimated to three temperatures, twelve, sixteen and twenty degrees Celsius. Twenty glass chambers were hung in three water baths, with one chamber in each bath left empty as that bath's blank. Five four-channel optode loggers read the oxygen in every chamber every five seconds, while one relay opened and closed the flush pumps on all of them together through the night. What the lab wants out of it is two figures for each fish: its standard metabolic rate and its routine metabolic rate, both in milligrams of oxygen per kilogram per hour.

`/app/RESPIROMETRY.md` is the lab's own note on how a night is worked up. It sets out how a logger reading becomes an oxygen concentration, which stretches of a record count as closed phases, how the blank is taken off and how the two rates are drawn from the night's phases. The chambers, the bath each hangs in, the volume of each and the fish it held with its mass are listed in `/app/bench_sheet.csv`. The loggers' exports are in `/app/optodes/`, the bath thermometers' records are in `/app/baths/`, the flush relay's record is in `/app/relay.log`, and the room barometer is in `/app/barometer.csv`.

The figures go in `/app/metabolic_rates.json`, as a list holding one object for each fish, in the form `{"fish": "T99", "smr": 61.4, "rmr": 88.2}`. A fish counts as right when both of its rates come within 1.5 per cent of the lab's own, and the night's work is accepted when no more than one of the seventeen fish is out.

You have 14400 seconds to complete this task. Do not cheat by using online solutions or hints specific to this task.
